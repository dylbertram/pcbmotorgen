# GetDP with Gmsh and Elmer FEM

Research date: 2026-10-18. Scope is the finite-depth PCB motor, nonlinear ferrite under permanent-magnet bias, aluminum eddy currents, motion drag and biased PWM impedance. This was source research only. No solver was installed or run. The existing app error figures supplied in the assignment are context, not independently checked measurements.

## Can either solver cover the required physics?

### Takeaway

Yes, both are credible external 3D FEM backends, not Rust libraries that drop into the existing analytical model. Elmer is my first proof-of-concept choice for the combined nonlinear, conducting, moving-material problem. GetDP is a strong alternative when owning and auditing the weak formulation is desirable. Neither investigation established a ready-made official example of the complete permanent-magnet-biased, tensor-incremental, 3D harmonic PCB impedance workflow.

### Cited Findings

#### GetDP and ONELAB

- GetDP explicitly supports nodal, edge, face and volume elements, 1D through 3D, steady, transient and harmonic regimes, with optional lumped circuits. Its problem definition is a user-written ASCII `.pro` file corresponding to a weak formulation. This is implemented FEM infrastructure, not an electromagnetic device API. [Official overview](https://getdp.info/).
- The stable manual gives a gauged 3D magnetic vector potential formulation, a 3D or 2D magnetodynamic H-phi formulation, frequency-domain resolution, time-domain resolution and nonlinear time-domain resolution. The latter nests `GenerateJac` and `SolveJac` inside `IterativeLoop` inside `TimeLoopTheta`. These are concrete supported building blocks. [Stable manual, sections 7.8.3, 7.8.4 and 7.9.2 through 7.9.4](https://getdp.info/doc/texinfo/getdp.html).
- The official development tutorial explicitly solves a ferromagnetic electromagnet using linear or nonlinear material laws and Newton-Raphson or Picard iterations. The bonus tutorial writes the differential reluctivity tensor as `dh/db = I nu + 2 nu'(|b|^2) b b^T` and implements the tangent through `JacNL`. This proves nonlinear magnetic Jacobian support, not a complete bias-plus-AC application. [Development manual, tutorial 3 and bonus](https://getdp.info/dev/doc/texinfo/getdp.html).
- The still-accessible official ONELAB inductor input offers `Static`, `Time domain`, and `Frequency domain`. Its `Flag_NL` declaration is explicitly `ReadOnly (Flag_AnalysisType==2)`. The model README says it is a 2D and 3D inductor/core model. Thus the example does not establish ordinary nonlinear BH in a single-frequency solve. [Input source](https://onelab.info/files/inductor/inductor.pro), [README](https://onelab.info/files/inductor/README.txt).
- A GetDP developer explained in 2018 that the inductor case cannot simply combine nonlinear BH and frequency domain, and that an effective nonlinear magnetic curve would be needed. She said that effective-curve feature was not available then. Do not turn this dated statement into a universal 2026 claim that GetDP cannot do nonlinear frequency-domain formulations, nor confuse it with incremental small-signal linearization. [Developer response](https://onelab.info/pipermail/getdp/2018/002063.html).
- Official ONELAB `magnets.pro` is a genuine finite-volume 3D permanent-magnet example. It defines each magnet volume and boundary, rotated coercive field `hc`, remanence `br`, scalar and edge-vector potential formulations, and Maxwell stress force integrals over an air element layer. It writes global and XYZ force tables. [Executable formulation source](https://onelab.info/files/magnets/magnets.pro), [README](https://onelab.info/files/magnets/README.txt).
- That magnets example is evidence for 3D permanent magnets and forces, not for nonlinear ferrite plus moving conductors in the same model. The core-inductor nonlinear example and magnets example must be combined and verified. [Magnets source](https://onelab.info/files/magnets/magnets.pro), [inductor source](https://onelab.info/files/inductor/inductor.pro).
- Developer explanation of the magnets example confirms Maxwell stress with an auxiliary basis function whose gradient supplies the element-layer normal. The response cites the eggshell method and Arkkio method. [Developer explanation](https://onelab.info/pipermail/getdp/2017/001999.html), [expanded discussion and references](https://onelab.info/pipermail/getdp/2017/002000.html).
- Electric-machine examples have moving-band machinery and Maxwell-stress torque. A developer confirms `VirtualWork`, `InitMovingBand2D` and `MeshMovingBand2D` are built-in and describes adding virtual-work torque output. [Developer response with code](https://onelab.info/pipermail/getdp/2016/001893.html).
- The documented community explanation of those machine templates explicitly limits the moving band to 2D and first-order triangular elements. This is not evidence of a 3D translational sliding-interface workflow. [Detailed template explanation](https://onelab.info/pipermail/getdp/2020/002191.html).
- Stable manual section 8.3 supplies a complete 2D A-v eddy-current problem, global voltage and current unknowns, and both complex-frequency and time-domain resolutions. It establishes circuit-aware formulations but must not be cited as a finite-depth 3D housing benchmark. [Stable manual](https://getdp.info/doc/texinfo/getdp.html).

#### Elmer FEM

- Elmer has separate 3D `WhitneyAVSolver` and `WhitneyAVHarmonicSolver` implementations. Real solver code reads `H-B Curve`, performs nonlinear iterations and Newton-Raphson linearization, accepts `Magnetization` from either material or body force, and accepts both `Angular velocity` and `Lorentz velocity`. This verifies the mechanisms in one real solver family. [Real 3D solver source](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/src/modules/MagnetoDynamics/WhitneyAVSolver.F90).
- The official `mgdyn_bh` regression input selects `WhitneyAVSolver`, `Newton-Raphson Iteration = True`, maximum 30 nonlinear iterations, tolerance `1e-6`, and magnetic field/permeability postprocessing. It has a tetrahedral `mesh_tet` and supplied HB data. This is stronger evidence than a general feature list. [Test input](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/tests/mgdyn_bh/case.sif), [test directory](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/tree/devel/fem/tests/mgdyn_bh).
- The official `mgdyn_airgap_force` test uses 3D `WhitneyAVSolver`, `Magnetization 2 = B0/(mu_pm*mu0)`, `Calculate Nodal Forces = True` and component `Calculate Magnetic Force = True`. It therefore demonstrates 3D PM excitation and integrated force, not just a 2D motor analogy. [Test input](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/tests/mgdyn_airgap_force/case.sif).
- The harmonic solver assembles complex matrices, accepts complex magnetization, scalar or 3-by-3 reluctivity tensors, electrical conductivity and material velocity. In the inspected source, the tensor path checks tensor size and applies the tensor in assembly. [Harmonic solver source](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/src/modules/MagnetoDynamics/WhitneyAVHarmonicSolver.F90).
- Important distinction: its ordinary harmonic BH branch computes `B_ip` from the harmonic solution, `babs = sqrt(sum(abs(B_ip)**2))`, then evaluates `H-B Curve` divided by `babs`. That is not evidence that it imports the differential tensor from an independently solved DC permanent-magnet operating point. [Harmonic source, LocalMatrix BH branch](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/src/modules/MagnetoDynamics/WhitneyAVHarmonicSolver.F90#L1414-L1430).
- The Elmer 9.0 release added magnetic anisotropy via a complex-valued reluctivity tensor to the time-harmonic AV model, a surface-impedance boundary condition, and magnetic co-energy postprocessing. Release 8.4 explicitly added the Lorentz velocity term to 3D harmonic AV. These are released capabilities, not merely proposed development. [9.0 release notes](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/releases/tag/release-9.0), [8.4 release notes](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/ReleaseNotes/release_8.4.md).
- The real solver automatically enables scalar potential when moving material or applied current-density boundary conditions require it. Its source message is `Moving material triggers the use of scalar potential`. This matters because a local `sigma v x B` calculation alone does not enforce current continuity or charge redistribution. [Real solver initialization, lines 74 onward](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/src/modules/MagnetoDynamics/WhitneyAVSolver.F90#L74-L84).
- The official Faraday-disc verification is particularly relevant to closure. A rotating conducting disc in a uniform axial field develops an electric potential until `E + v x B = 0`, rather than sustaining the naive local motional current. The README states the exact potential difference `0.5 omega a^2 |B|` and explicitly describes the Eulerian moving-body formulation. [Verification explanation](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/tests/mgdyn_faraday_disc/Readme.txt), [runnable SIF](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/tests/mgdyn_faraday_disc/faraday_disc.sif).
- Official `mgdyn_angular_steady` enables current density, electric field, Joule heating and nodal forces with an angular-velocity body force. It is useful adjacent evidence for motion-induced currents and losses, but its magnetization entries are commented out. Do not describe this input as a demonstrated permanent-magnet drag model. [Test source](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/tests/mgdyn_angular_steady/case.sif).
- Official `mgdyn_harmonic_wire` states that electric potential is solved alongside magnetic potential and supplies voltage boundary conditions, current density, electric field and Joule heating. This is concrete harmonic current-closure evidence. [Harmonic wire input](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/tests/mgdyn_harmonic_wire/case.sif).
- Official `circuits_harmonic_massive_flux_linkage` uses Cartesian 3D, a massive aluminum conductor at `3.07e7 S/m`, `CircuitsAndDynamicsHarmonic`, `WhitneyAVHarmonicSolver`, and `Calculate Flux Linkage = True`. This gives a direct starting point for terminal impedance and flux linkage without assuming a lossless energy-derived inductance. [Regression input](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/tests/circuits_harmonic_massive_flux_linkage/sif/1962.sif).
- Official `circuits_harmonic_stranded_homogenization` demonstrates a 3D harmonic circuit, complex anisotropic material coefficients, current density, Joule heating and homogenization loss. Its example coefficient comment says some values are not physical, so it is a regression demonstration, not validation of an actual winding. [Regression input](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/tests/circuits_harmonic_stranded_homogenization/sif/2241.sif).
- Elmer has regression examples for 3D transient fields and transient losses. The shared real WhitneyAV solver contains nonlinear BH and PM assembly, so nonlinear transient PM/eddy-current assembly is supported at source level. I did not locate a single inspected regression case exercising all of those features simultaneously. [Transient case](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/tests/mgdyn_transient/MGDynamicsTransient.sif), [transient loss case](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/tests/mgdyn_transient_loss/MGDynamicsTransient.sif), [real solver source](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/src/modules/MagnetoDynamics/WhitneyAVSolver.F90).
- TEAM Workshop Problem 30 is an explicit Elmer benchmark. The official test says it starts from a 300 Hz harmonic solution and computes a transient solution to check consistency, and integrates torque using nodal forces. It explicitly selects `Cartesian 2d`. This validates useful machinery, but not this app's finite-depth 3D drag. [TEAM30 input](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/tests/TEAM30a_3ph_transient/TEAM30a_3ph_transient.sif).
- Elmer also maintains 3D rotating magnetic boundary/mortar tests. These establish motion-interface infrastructure, not an out-of-box translating PCB-motor model. [RotatingBCMagnetoDynamics test](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/tree/devel/fem/tests/RotatingBCMagnetoDynamics).

### Inferences

- For the application's present priorities, start with Elmer's real 3D nonlinear PM solve and force postprocessing, then add the conducting housing. It has unusually direct source and regression evidence for electric potential, motional electromotive force, harmonic circuits and loss. GetDP remains attractive for an explicitly maintained, mathematically auditable custom formulation.
- Neither a scalar permeability saved after DC bias nor a generic nonlinear harmonic BH solve is the required biased small-signal model. For isotropic reversible `H = nu(|B|) B`, the incremental reluctivity is `dH/dB = nu I + (dnu/d|B|)/|B| B B^T`, evaluated at the DC operating point. GetDP's tutorial explicitly documents this tangent structure; Elmer's harmonic tensor assembly supplies a way to consume such a coefficient. Reusing the tensor across the two solves still needs an application-specific implementation and verification.
- Use terminal `Z(omega)=V/I` to define effective AC resistance and `L_eff=Im(Z)/omega`. Use incremental flux-linkage derivatives for low-frequency differential inductance. Do not substitute `2W/I^2` in a biased nonlinear permanent-magnet device and call it differential inductance.
- Compute drag by solving the conductor potential and integrating `J x B` or a suitable Maxwell-stress surface. Check mechanical power against Joule loss in the steady isolated-conductor case. A uniform-field Faraday-disc case is a useful negative test because naive local-current models predict the wrong current.
- Model a finite housing with its real ends, gaps and electrical connections. A 2D infinite-depth pass cannot establish 3D current return or end effects. Fixed-geometry Eulerian velocity terms can suit a steady translating conductor only where the chosen frame leaves geometry and material distribution stationary. A finite housing moving relative to finite magnets generally needs position-dependent or transient geometry treatment.
- Ordinary BH saturation is not ferrite minor-loop dynamics, complex incremental permeability, hysteresis or temperature-dependent loss. These need measured material data and, if required, a separate dynamic constitutive law. Permanent-magnet coercivity/remanence input is not proof of irreversible demagnetization support.

### Gaps

- No inspected official example completed nonlinear PM DC bias, spatially resolved differential tensor transfer, complex 3D eddy solve, and terminal PWM impedance together. Treat that coupling as custom model work in either solver, not a checkbox.
- No independently verified 3D drag benchmark matching finite-depth block magnets, thin ferrite and a finite aluminum housing was found in this bounded search. Neither solver can be declared accurate to the app's target without new validation.
- GetDP's current GitLab raw/template URLs returned an authentication page, even with HTTP 200. Its site says unauthenticated full repository access is now restricted because of scraping. The older official `onelab.info/files/` model sources remain accessible. Therefore current template internals were not assumed identical to the older downloadable models.
- Elmer `devel` source is mutable. Pin and check the chosen release before implementation. Source paths cited here were inspected during research; not every source feature was separately checked against release 26.2.

## Can a Rust desktop app run and redistribute the solvers?

### Takeaway

Use sidecar executables with generated model files and explicit version pinning. GetDP/Gmsh has the cleaner advertised binary coverage, including Apple Silicon development GetDP builds. Elmer has Linux and Windows distribution paths and macOS build instructions, but macOS packaging is a separate risk. GPL obligations remain relevant when binaries are bundled even if the Rust app communicates through files.

### Cited Findings

- GetDP's official command-line tutorial is `gmsh electromagnet.geo -2` followed by `getdp electromagnet.pro -solve Mag -pos Map`. This requires no GUI or ONELAB server. Gmsh itself supports CLI, `.geo` scripts and C/C++/Python/Julia/Fortran APIs. [GetDP tutorial](https://getdp.info/dev/doc/texinfo/getdp.html), [Gmsh overview](https://gmsh.info/).
- GetDP's advertised stable release is 3.5.0, dated 2022-05-13. It provides Linux, Windows and macOS x86 binaries. Development snapshots additionally provide macOS ARM. The page states Windows 10 minimum, glibc 2.24 stable or 2.28 development, macOS 10.15 x86 or 12 ARM, PETSc/MUMPS in binary releases, and MPI builds from source. A 2022 stable release should not be mistaken for an abandoned codebase, since the site also supplies current development snapshots and a 4.0 development manual. [Official downloads and requirements](https://getdp.info/).
- Gmsh's advertised stable release is 4.15.2 dated 2026-03-24, with Windows, Linux, macOS x86 and ARM binaries and SDKs. Gmsh is a mesher, not the nonlinear magnetic solver. [Official downloads](https://gmsh.info/).
- Elmer's latest release returned by the official GitHub release API was `release-26.2`, published 2026-04-28. Its release notes describe about 117 non-merge commits over Jan 19 to Apr 24, 2026. This is concrete recent maintenance evidence. [Release 26.2](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/releases/tag/release-26.2).
- Elmer's official README links Windows installers, Ubuntu PPA, WSL distribution, Linux/Windows/macOS compilation instructions and containers. The GitHub 26.2 release assets are source archives, not a complete set of desktop installers. [Official README](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/README.adoc), [release](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/releases/tag/release-26.2).
- Its macOS instructions explicitly warn that they may be out of date, use Homebrew GCC and CMake, and make MPI and ElmerGUI optional. Do not promise a maintained signed/notarized native Apple Silicon binary from this evidence. [Official macOS build instructions](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/compilation_instructions/macOS.md).
- ElmerGrid reads Gmsh meshes and can extrude structured 2D meshes to hexahedra or prisms, transform meshes and partition them. It is a separate command-line conversion stage. [ElmerGrid README](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/elmergrid/README.md).
- GetDP is GPL version 2 or later. Its site explicitly warns against integrating its code into closed-source distributed software and offers alternative licensing discussions. Gmsh is GPL version 2 or later with an external-library linking exception and commercial licensing. [GetDP licensing](https://getdp.info/#Licensing), [Gmsh licensing](https://gmsh.info/#Licensing).
- Elmer's licensing policy says libelmersolver and some libraries/modules are LGPL while ElmerGUI, ElmerGrid and most physics modules are GPL. It warns optional dependencies may impose further restrictions. The policy's opening paragraph reverses familiar GPL/LGPL version numbers, so inspect actual component headers and license files rather than copying that sentence. The inspected WhitneyAV files contain LGPL 2.1-or-later headers, and the harmonic source also contains a later GPL 2-or-later subroutine header. A blanket claim that the entire magnetic backend is LGPL is unsafe. [Licensing policy](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/license_texts/ElmerLicensePolicy.md), [harmonic source](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/src/modules/MagnetoDynamics/WhitneyAVHarmonicSolver.F90), [repository license](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/LICENSE.md).

### Inferences

- Rust integration should generate geometry, physical region tags and materials; spawn mesher/converter/solver; capture logs and convergence; parse numeric tables; and return failure instead of a plausible result after nonconvergence. A dedicated working directory, cancellation, timeout, cache keys and immutable material/version metadata are application work, not reasons to embed the solver through FFI.
- A minimal GetDP pipeline is `gmsh model.geo -3`, then `getdp model.pro -solve <resolution> -pos <postoperation>`. An Elmer pipeline is Gmsh mesh generation, ElmerGrid conversion and `ElmerSolver case.sif`. Confirm exact flags and mesh format compatibility against the pinned distributions before shipping.
- Prefer user-installed sidecars for the first prototype. If bundling, ship appropriate notices and corresponding-source arrangements for the exact binaries, modifications and dependencies. Process separation can make independent-program boundaries clearer but does not automatically exempt the app or bundle from GPL analysis. This is not legal advice. Obtain a distribution review before closed-source commercial packaging.
- Build and test a serial headless package first. MPI/PETSc/MUMPS/OpenMP and Fortran runtime combinations multiply packaging and numerical-reproducibility work, especially on Windows and macOS.

### Gaps

- No binaries were installed, launched or benchmarked. Startup behavior, Windows paths, cancellation, memory use, exact runtime dependencies, signing/notarization and Apple Silicon performance remain untested.
- This review did not audit every redistributed dependency's license or reach a legal conclusion about the current Rust app's distribution model.

## What is a practical proof of concept, including meshing and validation?

### Takeaway

Do not start with full PWM transient motion. First prove that a finite-depth nonlinear PM/ferrite force calculation removes the existing thick-attraction failure. Then separately validate closure and 3D drag, and finally build the tensor-incremental harmonic impedance solve. Keep the analytical path for rapid interaction and use FEM for reference calculations or cached corrections.

### Cited Findings

- Gmsh's scripting and API support lets the app own repeatable parameterized CAD and physical tagging rather than depend on manual GUI meshing. Its official manual covers extrusion, structured/transfinite meshing and mesh-size fields. [Overview](https://gmsh.info/), [Gmsh manual](https://gmsh.info/doc/texinfo/gmsh.html).
- Elmer has a specific harmonic thin-sheet regression with `Thin Sheet Thickness`, `Thin Sheet Relative Permeability` and `Thin Sheet Electric Conductivity`. Its inspected example uses constant material parameters and is not evidence that an arbitrarily saturated ferrite shell is handled correctly. [Thin-sheet input](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/tests/mgdyn_thinsheet_harmonic/thinsheet.sif).
- Elmer also has harmonic wire impedance-boundary regressions, and 9.0 officially released a harmonic AV surface-impedance condition. These provide potential later reductions for skin-depth-dominated metal, not justification to skip finite-thickness validation. [Impedance boundary test](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/tree/devel/fem/tests/mgdyn_harmonic_wire_impedanceBC), [9.0 notes](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/releases/tag/release-9.0).
- A published ONELAB teaching study describes three linear 3D magnetostatic examples and a nonlinear workshop problem, compared against analytical and experimental data, and cites TEAM13. This is supporting evidence for 3D nonlinear use, but the abstract alone does not establish this app's geometry or error target. [Authors' publication DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ROPEC.2017.8261649).
- A 2017 small-signal FEM paper reports agreement between full 3D nonlinear time-domain and 3D small-signal models with fully resolved lamination eddy currents. The retrieved abstract does not establish which exact public solver/template implements the workflow, so it supports the method, not an out-of-box GetDP/Elmer capability claim. [Institutional repository paper](https://lirias.kuleuven.be/retrieve/e59cc9db-f761-42d6-b43c-7f42aac5fa0f).

### Inferences

1. Establish a deterministic 3D geometry containing block PMs, actual magnet depth, actual ferrite thickness, air gap and an outer air domain. Use measured remanence/recoil permeability and a monotone BH curve appropriate to the ferrite grade and temperature. Begin with no conducting housing and no drive current.
2. Recover the analytical free-space block-magnet field and force in linear/no-ferrite cases. Add nonlinear ferrite and compare force versus magnet depth, gap and position against both the current app and available physical measurements. Include a deliberately linearized high-permeability ferrite case to isolate saturation from 3D geometry errors.
3. Converge outer-air size, gap mesh, ferrite thickness mesh and force integration independently. Prefer controlled extrusion/prism or hex layers where feasible. Avoid allowing very thin ferrite to force isotropically tiny tetrahedra across the entire air region. No fixed number of elements through the layer is defensible without convergence checks.
4. Add an isolated finite aluminum volume with no terminal current. Verify harmonic skin/eddy loss against an analytic or published case, and run the Faraday-disc zero-current/potential test as a closure check. Inspect current streamlines and integrated flux through conductor cuts, not only the norm of B.
5. Solve one slow steady-motion or short transient drag case with the real 3D ends and housing cuts. Reverse velocity to test drag sign, use zero velocity to test vanishing motional eddies, and check force/loss energy consistency. Do not reuse a 2D rotating motor benchmark as the acceptance result.
6. At one fixed position, solve the nonlinear PM plus DC-current operating point. Construct the spatial differential reluctivity tensor, preserving its bias direction, and feed it into the harmonic solve on the same mesh if possible. The PM bias is not an oscillating magnetization source in that AC perturbation solve.
7. Drive one small sinusoidal terminal current, extract terminal voltage, flux linkage, Joule loss and field distribution, then sweep frequency and DC bias. Check the AC limit against finite differences of nonlinear DC flux linkage and against small-amplitude nonlinear transient solutions. Only after this agreement should PWM harmonics be synthesized.
8. State the small-signal limit in the UI and result metadata. If ripple crosses a saturation knee or geometry changes appreciably during a PWM cycle, require nonlinear transient analysis rather than adding independent harmonic solutions. Ferrite high-frequency minor-loop loss remains a material-model problem even after correct tangent linearization.
9. Record solve duration, peak memory, element/DOF count and convergence failures for each stage. No runtime estimate is justified from this source-only review. Decide afterward whether an end-user solver mode is viable or FEM should remain an offline calibration/verification tool.

### Gaps

- Exact PCB trace terminal topology and ferrite BH/complex incremental material data were not supplied in this assignment. These determine whether full solid-conductor, stranded/homogenized, thin-sheet or circuit-coil representations are appropriate.
- Neither full-motion drag nor biased PWM impedance should be advertised as validated until the proposed independent checks pass. The assignment's existing 14–52% saturation attraction discrepancy and approximately 2% 2D eddy agreement do not resolve the missing 3D tests.
- Final recommendation is conditional: choose Elmer for the first combined physics proof of concept, retain GetDP as the auditable custom-formulation alternative, and retain the existing analytical solver for fast previews. This is an engineering recommendation from the evidence above, not a measured accuracy or performance ranking.
