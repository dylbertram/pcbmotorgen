# Nonlinear magnetic solver options and frameworks

Research date: 2026-10-18. Scope is NGSolve/Netgen, Sparselizard, MFEM and FEniCSx for a 3D PCB motor with permanent magnets, saturating back iron and conducting finite-depth parts. No dependencies were installed and no solver was run. These notes assess actual public formulations, not numerical validation of a new motor model. Existing magba limitations and rejection of Fenris/sim-femm are assignment constraints, not independently investigated here.

## Which candidates have relevant formulations rather than only general FEM machinery?

### Takeaway

Shortlist Sparselizard and NGSolve/Netgen. Sparselizard has the clearest inspectable, reusable 3D A-v eddy-current example plus separate saturation and rotating permanent-magnet examples. NGSolve has a strong 3D permanent-magnet/coil foundation and an industrial nonlinear eddy-current application, but I did not find a public turnkey source combining those requirements. MFEM and FEniCSx remain credible framework choices, not ready motor solvers.

### Cited Findings

#### Sparselizard

- **Supplied 3D eddy-current formulation.** `examples/magnetodynamics-av-induction-3d/main.cpp` solves a five-turn, finite-volume copper coil around an aluminium tube. It uses `field a("hcurl", {2,3}, spantree)` and `v("h1", {2,3})`, a tree gauge, 50 Hz voltage terminals, and the coupled equations `curl(nu curl A) + sigma(dt(A)+grad(v)) = Js` and `div(sigma(dt(A)+grad(v)))=0`. It writes B, E and J. The source explicitly says it was adapted from and validated against a GetDP example. This is genuine induced-current and skin-effect source, not a magnetostatic illustration. [Official source](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/examples/magnetodynamics-av-induction-3d/main.cpp)
- **Supplied nonlinear saturation formulation, but 2D.** `magnetostatics-vector-potential-saturation-2d` tabulates measured H-B data through 2.4 T, constructs cubic splines for H(B) and nu(B), evaluates nu using `norm(b)`, and iterates to a relative residual of 1e-5. It explicitly calls the model static anhysteretic saturation. The source says 3D needs minor adaptations, but the published example actually uses scalar Az and 2D geometry. Its update uses scalar `dhdb` times the vector increment, not the full differential reluctivity tensor discussed below. [Official saturation source](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/examples/magnetostatics-vector-potential-saturation-2d/main.cpp)
- **Supplied permanent magnets, motion and torque, but magnetostatic 2D.** The PMSM source rotates a rotor mesh through angular positions, adds a remanent-B vector, couples nonmatching rotor/stator meshes through a mortar continuity condition, applies antiperiodicity, and computes torque by virtual work. It also includes an Arkkio-formula alternative. Its permeability is constant; saturation is only suggested in a comment. It does not solve motion-induced eddy currents. Multiplying the 2D torque by a motor length is not a finite-depth 3D current-closure treatment. [Official PMSM source](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/examples/electric-motor-pmsm-2d/main.cpp)
- **Supplied 3D magnetic basis.** A separate 3D vector-potential magnetostatics example supplies tree gauging and a magnetic steel cylinder near a prescribed current. This is a better starting point for the 3D DC problem than merely extruding Az. [Official 3D source](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/examples/magnetostatics-vector-potential-3d/main.cpp); [official example descriptions](https://www.sparselizard.org/)
- **Native expression support.** The public expression implementation constructs general matrix-valued expressions, including symmetric matrices, and contains multiharmonic interpolation/Fourier handling. A 3x3 incremental reluctivity tensor is therefore implementable in the open library; the ready magnetic examples do not supply the complete biased-tensor harmonic workflow. [Expression source](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/src/expression/expression.cpp)

#### NGSolve/Netgen

- **Supplied 3D permanent-magnet formulation.** Official Maxwell tutorial constructs an OCC magnet and air volume, generates a curved 3D mesh, uses `HCurl(order=3, nograds=True)`, and solves the weak form `integral(mu^-1 curl(A) curl(v)) = integral(M curl(v))`. B is `curl(A)`. The example uses constant regional permeability and prescribed magnetization. This verifies permanent magnets and full vector fields, not BH saturation or eddy-current capability by itself. [Official tutorial](https://docu.ngsolve.org/latest/i-tutorials/unit-2.4-Maxwell/Maxwell.html)
- **Supplied realistic 3D current path.** The official coil notebook constructs a helical solid with leads using OCC, solves electric conduction on the coil volume with H1 potential and current injection, then solves the HCurl magnetostatic problem with that calculated current. This is relevant evidence for finite-width, finite-depth current paths. It remains DC and lacks induced-current feedback. [Official notebook source](https://docu.ngsolve.org/latest/jupyter-files/wta/coil.ipynb); [rendered tutorial](https://docu.ngsolve.org/latest/i-tutorials/wta/coil.html)
- **Native nonlinear machinery.** The official nonlinear tutorial applies a residual and calls `AssembleLinearization(gfu.vec)` to assemble the derivative, then demonstrates Newton solvers and damping. These are general PDE tools, not a supplied magnetic BH material model. [Official nonlinear notebook](https://docu.ngsolve.org/latest/jupyter-files/unit-3.3-nonlinear/nonlinear.ipynb)
- **Documented nonlinear 3D eddy-current application, not released formulation source.** Joachim Schöberl's official PDESoft 2024 presentation describes Siemens Energy transformers with time-harmonic low-frequency fields, saturation in the casing, eddy currents, layered-core homogenization and thin-shield anisotropic elements. It reports daily industrial use and 20-120 minute runs on strong desktop PCs for those models. The page shows results and model descriptions, not reusable nonlinear eddy-current solver code. Do not transfer that runtime to a PCB motor. [Author's transformer presentation](https://jschoeberl.github.io/talk-pdesoft/transformer/transformer.html)
- **Native implementation stack.** The same author describes C++17, pybind11 Python bindings, thread-based TaskManager, MPI, expression trees and optional runtime code generation; it also identifies CERBSim support and applications. [Author's software overview](https://jschoeberl.github.io/talk-pdesoft/talk_pdesoft.html)

#### MFEM

- **Supplied relevant miniapps, not just curl elements.** Official `tesla` magnetostatics includes prescribed permanent magnetization and current rings. `joule` solves transient low-frequency eddy-current diffusion plus one-way Joule heating, with scalar-potential, electric-diffusion and thermal-diffusion solves using AMG/AMS/ADS. These are meaningful starting formulations. However the documented materials use constant or piecewise material values; neither miniapp provides the combined nonlinear BH permanent-magnet moving-motor application. [Official EM miniapps and equations](https://mfem.org/electromagnetics/)
- **Source corroboration.** `joule.cpp` explicitly describes eddy-current equations, voltage terminals, material-tag changes, implicit integration options and rod/coil example meshes. The developer must edit boundary/material setup for a new geometry. This is a C++ application development effort rather than a reusable motor input deck. [Official Joule source](https://github.com/mfem/mfem/blob/master/miniapps/electromagnetics/joule.cpp)

#### FEniCSx

- **Magnetics tutorial is much narrower than the target.** Jørgen Dokken's maintained DOLFINx tutorial explicitly assumes an isotropic linear material, reduces the problem to 2D scalar Az, uses fixed permeability by region, and solves a Poisson equation for wires around iron. It does not establish nonlinear 3D motor, permanent-magnet or eddy-current readiness. This is author-maintained teaching material, not a built-in DOLFINx magnetic solver. [Tutorial source](https://github.com/jorgensd/dolfinx-tutorial/blob/main/chapter3/em.py)
- **Native electromagnetic discretization verified.** The official DOLFINx half-loaded-waveguide demo uses a mixed RTCE/Lagrange discretization, curl forms, PETSc and SLEPc, and verifies eigenvalues against analytical modes. That demonstrates edge-element EM infrastructure but is a waveguide modal problem, not a low-frequency A-v eddy-current solver. [Official demo source](https://github.com/FEniCS/dolfinx/blob/main/python/demo/demo_half-loaded-waveguide.py)

### Inferences

- Sparselizard wins on public formulation completeness for this shortlist. Combine its 3D A-v conductor model with a new 3D nonlinear DC material solve and explicit tensor linearization. The individual examples are useful, but their combination still requires electromagnetic engineering. [A-v source](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/examples/magnetodynamics-av-induction-3d/main.cpp); [saturation source](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/examples/magnetostatics-vector-potential-saturation-2d/main.cpp)
- NGSolve is a strong alternative when Python model development, integrated OCC meshing and LGPL core terms matter. Its nonlinear eddy-current industrial evidence makes it more than a speculative generic FEM choice, but a released, reproducible formulation or paid specialist assistance is a selection gate. [3D magnet tutorial](https://docu.ngsolve.org/latest/i-tutorials/unit-2.4-Maxwell/Maxwell.html); [industrial application](https://jschoeberl.github.io/talk-pdesoft/transformer/transformer.html)
- MFEM deserves preference over FEniCSx if building a C++ EM application from scratch because its Tesla/Joule miniapps already expose relevant sources and low-frequency diffusion. FEniCSx is reasonable for a team already proficient in UFL/DOLFINx, not the shortest path solely because it can express the PDEs. [MFEM miniapps](https://mfem.org/electromagnetics/); [DOLFINx demo](https://github.com/FEniCS/dolfinx/blob/main/python/demo/demo_half-loaded-waveguide.py)

### Gaps

- No inspected public example solves the complete 3D PCB motor with saturating back iron, permanent magnets, biased harmonic PWM impedance and moving-magnet eddy drag.
- No public, runnable NGSolve nonlinear magnetic A-v source was verified in this research. The Siemens application proves an implementation exists, not that its formulation, materials or preconditioner are shipped publicly.
- No reviewed example supplies measured anisotropic vector BH, hysteresis, irreversible magnet demagnetization or validated core-loss coefficients for the intended materials.

## What custom physics and validation are still required?

### Takeaway

All four can support development of the required equations, but none of the inspected examples is a drop-in backend. The difficult work is a correct constitutive tangent, charge-conserving 3D conductor topology, motion, extraction of terminal quantities, and validation. These cannot be replaced by a permeability scalar or a series of static rotor snapshots.

### Cited Findings

- The verified A-v example computes `E=-dt(A)-grad(v)` and `J=sigma E` in the full conducting volumes, including an electrically isolated aluminium tube. Its scalar-potential equation is the mechanism that permits induced currents to redistribute while satisfying current continuity. [Sparselizard 3D A-v source](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/examples/magnetodynamics-av-induction-3d/main.cpp)
- NGSolve's 3D permanent-magnet tutorial defines B as curl A and evaluates the full vector. Sparselizard's saturation example uses the norm of B, not an in-plane-only scalar component, although its actual field is 2D. [NGSolve vector formulation](https://docu.ngsolve.org/latest/i-tutorials/unit-2.4-Maxwell/Maxwell.html); [Sparselizard saturation](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/examples/magnetostatics-vector-potential-saturation-2d/main.cpp)
- The Sparselizard motor example supplies remanent magnetization and static torque, whereas the 3D induction example supplies AC eddy currents. They are different analyses. Neither source contains a moving-conductor velocity term. [Motor source](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/examples/electric-motor-pmsm-2d/main.cpp); [induction source](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/examples/magnetodynamics-av-induction-3d/main.cpp)

### Inferences

The following is proposed custom engineering, not a claim of shipped features. It follows from the sourced vector-potential and A-v equations above.

1. **Nonlinear DC bias.** Solve for the full 3D B0 with permanent-magnet remanence, coil DC currents and H(B) in the back iron. For an isotropic anhysteretic law use `H(B)=nu(b) B`, `b=|B|`. Face-normal B must contribute to b. A face field is not a separate saturation correction, and an Az-only extrusion cannot represent arbitrary out-of-plane flux. Use measured anisotropic laws instead if the material demands them. The source foundations are the [3D magnet weak form](https://docu.ngsolve.org/latest/i-tutorials/unit-2.4-Maxwell/Maxwell.html) and [BH spline example](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/examples/magnetostatics-vector-potential-saturation-2d/main.cpp).
2. **Full differential tensor.** Differentiate the vector law at B0. For nonzero b, `D=dH/dB = nu I + (nu'(b)/b) B0 tensor B0`. Equivalently, longitudinal reluctivity is `dH/db` and transverse reluctivity is `H/b`. Regularize the zero-field limit. Scalar secant permeability and scalar `dH/db` applied in every direction are not equivalent to this tensor. Sparselizard's supplied scalar-dhdb update can be useful as an iteration but must not be advertised as the exact tensor small-signal model. NGSolve's [automatic linearization machinery](https://docu.ngsolve.org/latest/jupyter-files/unit-3.3-nonlinear/nonlinear.ipynb) and Sparselizard's [matrix expressions](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/src/expression/expression.cpp) are implementation mechanisms.
3. **Small-signal harmonic impedance.** Freeze the spatial tensor D from the nonlinear DC solution, then solve the complex or sine/cosine A-v perturbation problem at every requested frequency, with terminal/circuit constraints. Calculate Z from terminal V/I, including conductor and back-iron eddy loss. A PWM Fourier sum is valid only within the small-signal approximation about this bias. Large ripple or periodic saturation needs nonlinear transient or coupled harmonic balance, not independent linear harmonics. The supplied [A-v harmonic example](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/examples/magnetodynamics-av-induction-3d/main.cpp) provides the linear conductor part, not this bias transfer.
4. **Finite-depth current closure.** Mesh the actual conducting backplate, copper traces, leads, vias and conductive magnets where relevant. Include real slots, insulation and disconnected components. Enforce insulating normal-current boundaries and terminal currents/voltages. Disconnected conducting domains require treatment of scalar-potential reference/nullspaces. Do not prescribe independent cross-sectional currents and assume their 3D closure; verify current continuity and terminal current conservation. The [3D A-v tube/coil](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/examples/magnetodynamics-av-induction-3d/main.cpp) and [NGSolve solid coil](https://docu.ngsolve.org/latest/jupyter-files/wta/coil.ipynb) are relevant starting demonstrations.
5. **Moving-magnet drag.** For stationary conductors, a moving magnet produces a time-dependent field/source. If using a moving-conductor frame, formulate `J=sigma(-partial_t A-grad(v)+u cross B)` with consistent coordinate transformation. Choose a transient moving/sliding mesh or a correctly derived travelling-periodic formulation. Motion snapshots alone omit Faraday-induced currents. The [mortar PMSM source](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/examples/electric-motor-pmsm-2d/main.cpp) is interface evidence, not a completed 3D eddy-drag model. Do not double-count motion by adding a velocity term to an already transformed field equation.
6. **Loss, force and inductance.** Integrate average conductor loss as `0.5 integral(J* dot sigma^-1 J)` for peak phasors, or without 0.5 for RMS phasors. Resolve air-gap Maxwell stress or use a consistently derived virtual-work method for force/torque. Extract differential inductance from flux-linkage derivatives or tensor perturbation solves; nonlinear secant inductance is a different quantity. Hysteresis and excess core loss need additional material models/data, not merely Joule heating. The source includes [static virtual-work torque](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/examples/electric-motor-pmsm-2d/main.cpp), but not a checked nonlinear dynamic implementation.
7. **Validation before accepting performance.** Reproduce the upstream induction and saturation regression values; compare the magnet field against magba in a linear air case; use a conducting plate/tube with known skin response; check a closed versus slotted conductor; then validate rotor-speed drag and PWM impedance against experiment or an independently established solver. At each stage refine mesh, polynomial order, outer-air domain, skin-depth layers, and time/frequency resolution. Check terminal power against integrated losses and, where appropriate, mechanical drag power. These are proposed acceptance tests, not tests run here. Upstream numerical reference lines exist in the [induction](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/examples/magnetodynamics-av-induction-3d/main.cpp), [saturation](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/examples/magnetostatics-vector-potential-saturation-2d/main.cpp) and [motor](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/examples/electric-motor-pmsm-2d/main.cpp) examples.

### Gaps

- Mesh size, solve memory, time per rotor position and overall engineering schedule cannot be estimated responsibly without actual layer thicknesses, conductivity, frequency range, domain dimensions and error targets. None was benchmarked.
- A documented 2D rotor mortar condition does not establish robust 3D sliding-interface, restart and field-history handling. That remains a prototype milestone.
- Measured BH curves must cover the intended field range and have a physically sensible derivative. The supplied saturation example explicitly limits its solution to the tabulated 2.4 T range; it is not a general extrapolation policy.

## How do integration, licensing, commercial boundaries and maintenance affect selection?

### Takeaway

Use a separate solver process controlled by Rust, with a versioned mesh/material/job/result contract. NGSolve offers a natural Python worker; Sparselizard offers a compact C++ executable. GPL and LGPL obligations still matter when shipping workers and dependencies, and Sparselizard's commercial Allsolve must not be confused with the features demonstrated in public source.

### Cited Findings

- **Sparselizard integration and meshing.** Official documentation describes Linux, macOS and Windows support, C++ source/static-library builds and separate Python bindings. It accepts Gmsh `.msh`, Nastran `.nas` and other formats, mixed 3D elements, curved geometry, and ParaView/Gmsh result files. Its internal mesher is described as suitable for relatively simple 2D or extruded 3D geometry; complex geometry should use an external mesher. Linking the Gmsh API is optional. [Official overview and download instructions](https://www.sparselizard.org/); [current CMake instructions](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/README.md)
- **Sparselizard dependencies.** The public CMake setup includes BLAS, Gmsh, METIS, MPI, MUMPS, PETSc and SLEPc configuration. The current installation script downloads PETSc main and requests OpenBLAS, MUMPS, METIS and SLEPc with real scalars and serial MUMPS. This is not a one-file dependency-free binary build and an unpinned script is not a reproducible release recipe. [CMake source](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/CMakeLists.txt); [dependency script](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/install_external_libs/install_petsc.sh)
- **Sparselizard open/commercial boundary.** The open repository is GPL-2.0-or-later. The verified saturation, 3D A-v induction, permanent-magnet motor, tree gauging, harmonic fields and mortar example are public code, not evidence of a paid magnetic module requirement. Its official website separately advertises Quanscient Allsolve as a commercial solution built on and enhanced beyond Sparselizard, especially for large-scale simulations. Allsolve's vendor site describes cloud-native domain decomposition, APIs and commercial platform workflows. Do not transfer those product capabilities or performance claims to the open build. [Open license](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/LICENSE); [open examples and commercial referral](https://www.sparselizard.org/); [Allsolve product](https://quanscient.com/allsolve)
- **Important DDM qualification.** Sparselizard's own homepage also says general domain-decomposition methods are available, while promoting Allsolve for commercial large-scale DDM. Public source contains MPI wrappers. Therefore a blanket claim that all parallelism or all DDM is paid-only is not established by these sources. [Homepage](https://www.sparselizard.org/); [public MPI source](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/src/slmpi.cpp)
- **NGSolve and Netgen core terms.** Both repositories provide LGPL 2.1 license texts. This is different from Sparselizard's GPL core. OCC-based construction/meshing is directly demonstrated in the official magnetic tutorial. Exact binary-platform packaging and bundled-license obligations should be checked for the pinned release rather than assumed from a developer's machine. [NGSolve license](https://github.com/NGSolve/ngsolve/blob/master/LICENSE); [Netgen license](https://github.com/NGSolve/netgen/blob/master/LICENSE); [OCC magnetic example](https://docu.ngsolve.org/latest/i-tutorials/unit-2.4-Maxwell/Maxwell.html)
- **MFEM licensing/build.** MFEM itself is BSD-3-Clause. Its official installation file supports CMake/Make, Visual Studio and Xcode generators and many optional solver libraries, including hypre, METIS, SuiteSparse, MUMPS, PETSc-related integrations through application choices, and GPU tooling. Audit the actual selected dependency graph rather than treating MFEM's BSD license as covering every linked library. [Core license](https://github.com/mfem/mfem/blob/master/LICENSE); [build/dependency instructions](https://github.com/mfem/mfem/blob/master/INSTALL)
- **FEniCSx licensing/platforms.** DOLFINx's README explicitly states LGPL-3.0-or-later. Its repository includes both GPL and LGPL license texts; reading `COPYING` alone gives the wrong conclusion about the library's overall license. The README recommends macOS conda, Linux apt/Docker/conda and Windows Docker/WSL2, with native Windows conda in beta and PETSc unavailable there. It supplies real/complex container modes and amd64/arm64 images. [Official README](https://github.com/FEniCS/dolfinx/blob/main/README.md)
- **Mesher/dependency caveats.** Gmsh is GPL-2.0-or-later with a specific linking exception, not a generally permissive proprietary-embedding license. OCC uses LGPL 2.1 with an additional exception. SuiteSparse packages/modules have separate licenses, including copyleft components and commercial alternatives, so selecting `umfpack` or another packaged inverse changes the audit. [Gmsh license appendix](https://gmsh.info/doc/texinfo/gmsh.html#License); [OCC licensing](https://dev.opencascade.org/resources/licensing); [SuiteSparse license collection](https://github.com/DrTimothyAldenDavis/SuiteSparse/blob/dev/LICENSE.txt)
- **Maintenance snapshots.** At this research date, upstream commits were visible for NGSolve on 2026-10-18, MFEM on 2026-10-18 and DOLFINx on 2026-10-17. Sparselizard's latest returned commit was 2026-08-07, merging a public-header cleanup. This establishes recent activity, not support guarantees or equal maintainer capacity. [NGSolve snapshot](https://github.com/NGSolve/ngsolve/commit/6efed66dadd290683872265aa17a5f4b8e21ec7a); [MFEM snapshot](https://github.com/mfem/mfem/commit/54d7f64513464396faa5bf86ff68d64956315c9d); [DOLFINx snapshot](https://github.com/FEniCS/dolfinx/commit/fe1c859e3b87eb6101c7abf6a4f582195acbb488); [Sparselizard snapshot](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/commit/e536ff15905556400909b47c4ff3df405e288b44)

### Inferences

- Recommended Rust boundary: create a job directory containing SI-unit geometry, tagged mesh, BH tables, conductivity, remanence, terminals, rotor position/speed and excitation; invoke a pinned Python NGSolve worker or compiled Sparselizard worker; consume structured scalar/tensor results plus field files and a convergence manifest. Include exit status, tolerances, mesh hash, solver version and warnings. This avoids committing the Rust application to an unstable C++ ABI. It is an integration design proposal based on the [NGSolve Python architecture](https://jschoeberl.github.io/talk-pdesoft/talk_pdesoft.html) and [Sparselizard executable/output workflow](https://www.sparselizard.org/).
- A separate process is a useful engineering and packaging boundary, not an automatic GPL exemption. A distributed Sparselizard-based worker will generally need GPL-compliant source distribution; the independence of a proprietary Rust controller depends on how the combined product works. Keep ordinary file-based interfaces and obtain legal review. LGPL workers and linked libraries also require notices and compliance. [Sparselizard GPL text](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/LICENSE); [NGSolve LGPL text](https://github.com/NGSolve/ngsolve/blob/master/LICENSE)
- Start the prototype on Linux to reduce solver/dependency variation; then qualify macOS and Windows separately. In particular, the current Windows FEniCSx beta without PETSc is a poor basis for assuming identical harmonic/nonlinear production behavior. [DOLFINx platform guidance](https://github.com/FEniCS/dolfinx/blob/main/README.md); [Sparselizard platform/build options](https://www.sparselizard.org/)
- Selection gates: reproduce Sparselizard's 3D induction regression, implement and finite-difference-check the BH tensor, demonstrate one 3D biased backplate impedance result, then assess motion. In parallel, ask NGSolve maintainers/CERBSim for an open or supportable nonlinear A-v magnetic formulation. Do not choose solely on generic solver breadth. [Induction reference](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/examples/magnetodynamics-av-induction-3d/main.cpp); [NGSolve support pointer](https://github.com/NGSolve/ngsolve/blob/master/README.md)

### Gaps

- The precise open versus commercial availability of every Sparselizard DDM accelerator and modern Allsolve magnetic/motion workflow was not established. Obtain a version-specific feature/license statement. No evidence found that the public static saturation or harmonic A-v example needs a commercial module.
- NGSolve binary support for each intended OS/architecture, Python version and optional sparse solver was not fully verified. A failed documentation URL must not be treated as lack of platform support.
- Per-version licensing for PETSc, SLEPc, MUMPS, METIS, MPI implementations and the exact packaged solver binaries remains a release audit item. No all-dependencies-cleared legal conclusion is offered.
- No quoted support contracts, guaranteed response times, reliable staff counts or quantitative maintenance comparison were found.
