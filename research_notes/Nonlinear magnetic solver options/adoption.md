# Nonlinear saturation and eddy currents: build or integrate?

Research date: 2026-10-18. This is a source-based adoption assessment, not a solver trial. No software was installed and no simulations were run. Relative project links below point to the local evidence inspected for this assignment. Proposed requirements and engineering judgments are separated from vendor-documented capabilities.

## Which existing solvers address the actual gaps?

### Takeaway

Keep magba for interactive prescribed-source fields and keep real FEMM 4.2 for independent 2D verification. Procure a small, reproducible 3D reference study in Maxwell, COMSOL AC/DC, or the current Altair Flux solver through SimLab before committing to a production integration. A new general-purpose 3D Rust magnetic solver is the wrong first response to the present accuracy failures.

### Cited findings

- The project's assessment identifies magba as an analytical prescribed-source B-field backend, not a self-consistent soft-material or induced-current solver. It finds neither fenris nor sim-nest/sim-femm delivers the demonstrated magnetic capabilities required here. This assessment is existing project research, not a fresh source audit in this assignment. [Local library assessment](../../reports/Magnetic%20solver%20library%20assessment.md)
- The failed thick saturation cases are concrete. For 5 mm flexible ferrite, attraction is 51.9% high and the coil field ratio is 12.9% high. For 2 mm MnZn, attraction is 13.5% high. Supplying the model's own two-slope B-H curve to FEMM leaves essentially the same force errors. The recorded diagnosis is normal-field saturation at the plate face, absent from the model's in-plane saturation treatment. Thin 0.1, 0.2 and 1 mm ferrite cases fare much better. [Local saturation tables and discussion](../../scripts/femm/RESULTS.md#saturation-and-eddy-currents-2026-10-18)
- The 100 kHz checks agree within 0.5% on the real coil factor and within 1.3% on added resistance. Traveling-wave drag agrees within 2.1% in the tested planar cases. These use fixed permeability in the harmonic solve, including selected effective values in saturated cases. Finite 5 mm magnet depth, array-end eddy currents, coverage weighting, hysteresis/core loss and temperature-dependent conductivity are explicitly unverified. The recorded 0.86 N drag at 20 m/s is therefore not an established full-motor prediction. [Local harmonic results, discussion and limits](../../scripts/femm/RESULTS.md#coil-at-100-khz-2-sin-current-sheets-solved-alone-time-harmonic)
- Real FEMM 4.2 already supports nonlinear magnetostatic bias, AC incremental-permeability solutions linked to that bias with `mi_setprevious(filename,1)`, DC incremental and frozen-permeability analyses, and sliding-band rotor-position sweeps. Its author describes repeated nonlinear operating points followed by per-phase incremental solves and an external field-circuit wrapper. He does not present one incremental solve as a complete nonlinear transient simulator. [FEMM release notes and field-circuit workflow](https://www.femm.info/wiki/ReadMe42), [incremental AC purpose](https://www.femm.info/wiki/IncrementalPermeability)
- The local FEMM implementation is 2D planar with a depth normalization, not a mesh of the real finite-width motor. The magnet-bias solve followed by incremental coil excitation is already exercised locally. The harmonic drag reference replaces motion with a prescribed traveling current sheet. Those are useful independent 2D references, not independent 3D motion validation. [Local FEMM geometry and workflow](../../scripts/femm/RESULTS.md#setup), [saturation/eddy workflow](../../scripts/femm/RESULTS.md#femm-setup-for-this-section)
- Ansys Maxwell's 3D transient documentation explicitly supports nonlinear B-H material dependencies, permanent device motion including translation, arbitrary time-varying current/voltage excitation, circuit coupling and motion-induced eddy currents in stationary and moving parts. It uses edge and nodal unknowns, backward time stepping and Newton-Raphson. Reported outputs include force/torque, linkage, back EMF and losses. Translation requires remeshing the motion band, and the user remains responsible for a mesh that captures skin/proximity effects. This is a directly relevant nonlinear magnetic low-frequency solver, not an RF substitute. [Ansys Maxwell 2025 R2 3D transient formulation](https://ansyshelp.ansys.com/public/Views/Secured/Electronics/v252/en/Subsystems/Maxwell/Content/Maxwell/3DTransientExcitationsSources.htm)
- COMSOL AC/DC documents nonlinear B-H materials, permanent magnets, anisotropy and hysteresis models, 2D/3D frequency- and time-domain induction, motion, force/loss extraction, circuit coupling, inductance matrices, and linear-motion moving meshes. Its frequency-domain perturbation study explicitly computes a linearized response about a stationary bias solution. AC/DC is an add-on to COMSOL Multiphysics, and CAD/ECAD and other integration products can be separate purchases. [AC/DC capabilities and product dependencies](https://www.comsol.com/acdc-module), [bias plus perturbation study definition](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.comsol/comsol_ref_solver.36.059.html)
- Altair's current documentation says that since 2026 legacy Flux 3D and Flux PEEC are no longer available, directing new/imported 3D work to SimLab. SimLab's 3D Transient Magnetic application uses the Flux solver and documents permanent magnets, eddy currents, skin/proximity effects, circuit and kinematic coupling. Its page also lists version-specific missing functionality and legacy-export advice. A 2026 procurement must resolve these migration details rather than assume an old Flux 3D automation script remains supported. [Altair 2026 transition and application distinctions](https://help.altair.com/flux/Flux/Help/english/UserGuide/English/topics/CeQuonPeutModeliserAvecFlux.htm), [SimLab 3D transient scope and limitations](https://help.altair.com/simlab/help/en_us/topics/analysis/TransientMagnetic3D/Introduction_MT3D.htm)
- Altair distinguishes magnetostatic, transient magnetic and steady-state AC applications. Its overview explicitly forbids magnets in the ordinary steady-state AC application. Do not assume that application directly implements permanent-magnet bias plus incremental AC; demand a demonstrated supported workflow for the procured version. [Altair application selection](https://help.altair.com/flux/Flux/Help/english/UserGuide/English/topics/CeQuonPeutModeliserAvecFlux.htm)
- Palace is a substantial open-source full-wave electromagnetic solver with eigenmode, driven frequency/time-domain and static inductance-extraction modes. That makes it more than an RF cavity-only code. However, the inspected problem documentation establishes surface-current/flux-loop magnetostatics and full-wave driven transients, not nonlinear ferromagnetic B-H bias, biased incremental MQS, permanent-magnet motion or nonlinear eddy-brake validation. It is not an evidenced replacement for this missing magnetic workflow. [Palace scope](https://awslabs.github.io/palace/stable/), [documented problem types](https://awslabs.github.io/palace/stable/guide/problem/)
- GetDP is a more relevant open-source feasibility candidate than Palace for this particular question. Its manual supplies edge spaces, gauge conditions, multiply connected-domain treatment, 3D magnetostatic and magnetodynamic formulations, and nonlinear transient resolution examples. It is a formulation environment, not an already accepted PCB-motor backend. Its GPL and alternative commercial licensing require a distribution review. [GetDP scope and examples](https://getdp.info/doc/texinfo/getdp.html#Overview), [edge/gauge/topology examples](https://getdp.info/doc/texinfo/getdp.html#FunctionSpace-examples), [license discussion](https://getdp.info/doc/texinfo/getdp.html#Copying-conditions)

### Inferences

- Prefer Maxwell for the first procured moving-magnet nonlinear 3D drag reference if expertise and access are equal. Its published transient formulation is especially explicit about precisely the required motion, conductivity and B-H combination. COMSOL AC/DC is equally worth requesting a quotation and sample model from, particularly for custom material studies and bias-plus-frequency sweeps. Altair remains a credible alternative where the organization already uses its tools, but verify the SimLab version and API migration first. This is a fit judgment, not a measured accuracy or speed ranking.
- Purchase a reference study or use an existing licensed installation before buying an application-wide integration. A solver license buys numerical infrastructure, not this motor's geometry, material identification, validated forces or deployment rights. An expert-operated offline study can answer the current design question without making every desktop user buy a solver.
- Keep FEMM's nonlinear magnetostatic and bias-linearized 2D roles. It cannot resolve out-of-plane current closure or finite magnet depth merely by changing its planar depth parameter. Its sliding-band rotor feature also does not establish arbitrary translating finite 3D geometry or a general nonlinear MQS time integrator.
- If a native solver becomes a product requirement, evaluate a narrowly bounded 2D implementation first. Solving the planar vector field could address the thick normal-face saturation failure; it cannot settle finite-width drag.

### Gaps

- No commercial solver was trialed. Accuracy, runtime, memory, scripting reliability and support for this exact material/motion combination remain acceptance-test questions.
- No current prices or redistribution/server-use rights were established. Obtain written terms for local automation, remote jobs, concurrent licenses, vendor components and export of reusable result tables. Do not assume an external process avoids licensing obligations.
- The FEMM manual PDF could not be extracted by the fetch tool. The FEMM claims above rely on its fetched release notes and the locally exercised planar workflow. No capability claim is made for the separately advertised FEMM 5 download.
- GetDP merits a focused proof of concept, not a blanket maturity verdict for this application. No moving nonlinear 3D motor model, force benchmark or turnkey adapter was inspected.

## What workflow and architecture should the desktop application own?

### Takeaway

Use a fast analytical Rust path for interaction, an optional external high-fidelity job path for expensive solves, and provenance-tracked result tables or reduced models between them. Classify every result by dimensionality, bias, amplitude, motion and validation envelope. A harmonic answer is valid only for its stated linearized or explicitly approximate model.

### Cited findings

- FEMM's documented incremental workflow first solves a nonlinear operating point and then extracts incremental response, with separate phase excitations and a position/current-dependent field-circuit model. COMSOL makes the same stationary-bias/linearized-perturbation distinction explicit. [FEMM workflow](https://www.femm.info/wiki/ReadMe42), [COMSOL perturbation](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.comsol/comsol_ref_solver.36.059.html)
- Locally, incremental inductance varies strongly with coil phase relative to the magnet bias. For 2 mm MnZn, FEMM gives factors 1.107 and 1.588 at the two sampled alignments. An averaged scalar is not a complete position-dependent inductance model. [Local incremental table](../../scripts/femm/RESULTS.md#inductance-under-the-magnets-incremental-permeability-08-mm-pcb)
- The motor's reported electrical period is 12 mm. At 20 m/s its fundamental traveling-field frequency is about 1.667 kHz, whereas PWM is 100 kHz. Existing harmonic validation treats these separately. The same report identifies unmodeled hysteresis/core loss and fixed-permeability AC assumptions. [Local geometry](../../scripts/femm/RESULTS.md#setup), [traveling-wave frequencies](../../scripts/femm/RESULTS.md#eddy-drag-of-a-travelling-field-2-d-fundamental-per-b02mu0-of-the-bare-field-at-the-stack-top), [limits](../../scripts/femm/RESULTS.md#remaining-limits-of-backplanemodrs)
- Commercial solver documentation provides extraction mechanisms, but does not absolve the user of resolving the physics spatially or selecting the right study. Maxwell explicitly assigns mesh adequacy for transient skin/proximity effects to the user. COMSOL distinguishes homogenized coils, solid conductors, shells and surface approximations. [Maxwell transient guidance](https://ansyshelp.ansys.com/public/Views/Secured/Electronics/v252/en/Subsystems/Maxwell/Content/Maxwell/3DTransientExcitationsSources.htm), [COMSOL representations](https://www.comsol.com/acdc-module)

### Inferences

#### Select the analysis before selecting the executable

1. For standstill forces and thick-plate saturation, solve nonlinear magnetostatics at each required position and DC phase-current state. A single-valued B-H law models saturation, not hysteresis loss.
2. For small ripple around a nearly stationary bias, linearize the material law at that spatial bias and solve incremental MQS at the relevant ripple frequencies. For an isotropic memoryless B-H law, incremental permeability has longitudinal derivative `dB/dH` and transverse secant `B/H`; it is generally a tensor around a nonzero vector bias. Preserve the tensor or demonstrate that a simplification meets the error budget. Frozen/secant permeability is not interchangeable with the incremental derivative.
3. For PWM, a linearized model can use harmonic superposition of the actual current or circuit response spectrum, with frequency-dependent impedance and material losses. One 100 kHz solve is not an arbitrary PWM waveform. Small ripple means small change in material state, not merely high switching frequency. Check peak excursions near the B-H knee and demonstrate amplitude independence over the intended ripple range.
4. For moving magnets, the backplane sees a large varying bias even if PWM ripple is small. Linear traveling-wave modes are legitimate for constant material properties and compatible geometry. They do not reproduce a nonlinear material traversing a changing field over a pole pitch. Nonlinear transient MQS is needed when saturation changes materially during motion, eddy fields modify that saturation, finite entry/exit matters, or hysteretic history matters. Validated nonlinear periodic/harmonic-balance or multirate methods may replace brute-force stepping, but a single ordinary linear harmonic solve cannot.
5. When the low-frequency motion and high-frequency PWM scales separate, test a sequence of nonlinear slow bias states with local small-signal frequency sweeps. Use it only after comparison with a coupled transient case establishes that the neglected fast/slow interaction is acceptable. Do not freeze a magnetostatic bias when slow motion-induced currents already change that state appreciably.

These are engineering workflow requirements inferred from the documented bias/perturbation distinction and the local missing-physics evidence above, not claims that they have been implemented.

#### Proposed application boundary

- Retain the magba adapter and current analytical backplane estimator as the default fast screening path. Flag thick near-knee attraction and unvalidated 3D drag rather than returning a solver-independent accuracy promise.
- Add a versioned neutral problem description with SI units, axes, magnet polarization and recoil law, actual routed winding terminals/series connections, material regions, electrical insulation/contact rules, nonlinear B-H data, conductivity versus temperature, boundaries, periodicity and requested outputs. Geometry export alone is insufficient.
- Run optional vendor or open-source jobs through a process or remote-worker boundary. The Rust app submits a reproducible package, tracks progress/cancellation, captures logs and imports typed results. Keep vendor scripting runtimes and licenses out of the default interactive path. Begin with offline import/export if unattended automation has not yet passed acceptance.
- Own the model/material/mesh adapter even with a mature solver. It must distinguish copper from FR4, represent thin ferrite/adhesive correctly, choose solid versus stranded windings, preserve 3D current return paths, resolve the gap and skin depth, and map solver region IDs back to motor components. A mature mesher still needs these rules and quality checks.
- Own the bias, harmonic, motion and transient orchestration, including which excitations remain active at each stage, position/current sweeps, tensor linearization, circuit coupling and stopping criteria. No solver-name switch can infer those decisions.
- Own force/loss extraction conventions. Request separate mover, coil and backplane forces, winding linkage and incremental inductance matrix, terminal complex impedance, Joule loss by region and separately modeled core losses. Check peak versus RMS phasor normalization. Do not infer total mover reaction by simply negating the coil force in a system with another magnetic/conducting body.
- For steady passive drag in a compatible reference frame, check mechanical input against Joule plus modeled magnetic loss. In transient or energized cases include field-energy change and electrical terminal power. Do not enforce `F*v = Joule loss` where the omitted terms are nonzero.
- Cache by canonical geometry, all material data/version hashes, temperature, position, bias currents, speed/frequency, excitation amplitude, solver/version, representation and convergence settings. Store raw fields or project artifacts for audit alongside integrated quantities.
- Start reduced models with bounded tables for force, linkage and impedance. Use coenergy/linkage-consistent interpolation where applicable, preserve passivity of fitted impedance models, and test held-out points. A frequency-dependent conductor model has memory; a scalar lookup of instantaneous force is not automatically a valid transient replacement. Mark extrapolation as unsupported and fall back to a job or a labeled estimate.

### Gaps

- Material identification may dominate the remaining error. The local flexible-sheet Bsat is borrowed from bulk material and its nominal thickness includes nonmagnetic tape/adhesive. Better FEM cannot turn those assumptions into measured material data. [Local material provenance](../../scripts/femm/RESULTS.md#what-the-model-does-now)
- The correct hardware PWM topology, current waveform and winding circuit must be settled before using a high-frequency field solution as a drive prediction. The local report already flags an unverified full-bus versus half-bus ripple assumption. [Local drive caveat](../../scripts/femm/RESULTS.md#benchmark-winding-dxf-slice)
- No quantitative crossover runtime or safe ripple amplitude is established. Derive those from representative models and amplitude/convergence sweeps, not a guessed universal threshold.

## Is a bespoke Rust solver feasible, and what gates should decide adoption?

### Takeaway

A limited 2D solver is feasible as a deliberately scoped numerical project. A reliable 3D nonlinear moving-conductor solver is a separate solver-development program, with substantially greater numerical, geometry and verification obligations than an external-solver adapter. Fund it only if deployment, licensing or repeated workloads justify that ownership after the reference studies.

### Cited findings

- GetDP's manual treats edge-element spaces, gauge conditions and multiply connected domains as explicit formulation concerns. Its nonlinear support distinguishes Picard iteration from Newton with a supplied `JacNL` term. These are concrete examples of work hidden by the phrase "use a FEM library." [Edge spaces and gauge](https://getdp.info/doc/texinfo/getdp.html#FunctionSpace-examples), [multiply connected domains](https://getdp.info/doc/texinfo/getdp.html#Multiply-connected-domains), [nonlinear expressions](https://getdp.info/doc/texinfo/getdp.html#Fields)
- Maxwell's mature 3D transient solver combines edge/nodal fields, circuit equations, Newton iteration, time stepping, motion-band treatment and force extraction. The documented implementation illustrates the breadth of the target, not a requirement that a Rust implementation copy its exact formulation. [Maxwell 3D transient internals](https://ansyshelp.ansys.com/public/Views/Secured/Electronics/v252/en/Subsystems/Maxwell/Content/Maxwell/3DTransientExcitationsSources.htm)
- The local assessment finds that adopting fenris would still leave electromagnetic formulation, B-H materials, boundary conditions, meshing integration, force extraction and verification to the project. Existing local 2D success does not validate 3D drag. [Local assessment](../../reports/Magnetic%20solver%20library%20assessment.md)

### Inferences

#### What full 3D ownership entails

- Choose and derive a compatible MQS formulation such as A-phi or T-omega, including sources, material interfaces, insulating regions and circuit constraints. Standard H(curl) edge elements are a usual choice for the vector field, with orientation-correct assembly and compatible scalar spaces. A proven alternative formulation is possible; ordinary nodal tetrahedra plus a generic Newton utility are not a sufficient plan.
- Handle gauge/nullspaces and conducting topology. Enforce charge conservation, current closure, zero normal current on insulated boundaries, terminal currents and floating conductor potentials as appropriate. Treat holes/cuts/cohomology or equivalent global constraints for the chosen formulation. Test disconnected and multiply connected conductors, not just a solid slab.
- Implement a vector nonlinear constitutive law and consistent material Jacobian. For isotropic `H = nu(|B|) B`, the tangent is `nu I + (dnu/d|B|)/|B| B B^T`, with a regular zero-field limit. Reassemble at the current Newton state, use damping/continuation and verify derivatives numerically. Smooth/interpolate measured B-H data without introducing nonphysical slopes. Add anisotropy and hysteresis only with explicit, validated laws and state evolution.
- Supply sparse real/complex assembly, appropriate preconditioners and robust iterative/direct solver integration. Low-frequency, high-permeability and high-conductivity limits can expose conditioning and nullspace errors. Rust does not remove these numerical issues; existing algebra libraries can reduce implementation work.
- Build stable transient stepping and nonlinear stopping logic, consistent motion terms or moving-mesh interfaces, circuit coupling, restart and error estimation. Resolve PWM edges only when that fidelity is necessary; validate any averaged or multirate substitute.
- Integrate a dependable mesher with physical-region tags, thin layers, air-gap resolution, skin-depth control, outer-domain treatment and motion interfaces. Geometry repair and mesh quality across user-generated PCB designs are ongoing product work.
- Validate force separately from field residual. Air-gap stress or virtual-work extraction needs mesh and displacement convergence, energy consistency and a defined body grouping. A converged linear solve does not establish converged force or accurate corner peak B.

These are proposed ownership tasks, informed by the cited formulations, not newly benchmarked claims. The main cost is specialist numerical engineering and sustained verification. No defensible completion date or staffing number follows from this desk research.

#### Narrower native 2D option

A planar scalar `A_z` solver with nodal triangles avoids much of the 3D curl/gauge complexity. Scope its first version to nonlinear magnetostatics with permanent-magnet excitation and force extraction. Then add bias-tangent complex MQS for prescribed planar currents, plus clear out-of-plane net-current constraints. Validate it against real FEMM, not only against the analytical model it is intended to improve. Arbitrary nonlinear transient behavior, axisymmetry and hysteresis would be separate additions. This could remove Wine from a selected 2D workflow and address normal-face saturation, but it is still new solver ownership and cannot close the real 3D drag gap.

#### Proof-of-concept gates, proposed rather than achieved

1. **Reproducible model and deployment.** Export the exact benchmark into the candidate tool without hand repair. Preserve units, winding connections, magnetic layer versus adhesive thickness and material data. Archive scripts, solver version and model. Demonstrate the intended OS/job path and obtain acceptable automation/distribution terms before production adoption.
2. **Recover established 2D results and the known failures.** Reproduce no-stack/linear limits, thin saturated sheets and the failed 5 mm flex/2 mm MnZn cases. Require the replacement to resolve vector normal-face saturation, not tune an effective scalar until one force matches. Reproduce the position-dependent incremental matrix and 100 kHz impedance separately.
3. **Numerical convergence.** Set the error budget first. A suggested initial screen is less than 2% change in integrated force, impedance and loss under mesh, time-step where applicable, and outer-domain refinement; then less than 5% disagreement with a trustworthy reference where its uncertainty permits. These are candidate gates, not promises. Treat near-zero force with an absolute tolerance and define a sampling rule for peak B rather than requiring convergence of an ideal sharp-corner singularity.
4. **Incremental validity.** At several bias positions, compare the tangent result to finite-amplitude nonlinear calculations. Sweep ripple amplitude and frequency across the relevant PWM spectrum. Reject small-signal use where material excursions, loss or impedance leave the declared error budget. Confirm that frozen and incremental permeability have not been confused.
5. **Finite 3D drag.** Recover a deep/periodic limit against existing FEMM traveling-wave results, then solve the real 5 mm magnet depth and finite array with closed 3D conducting current paths. Sweep width, array length, speed and housing thickness. Include 1, 20 and 100 m/s where physically relevant. Establish end-effect corrections rather than assuming the static cross-travel correction transfers to drag.
6. **Nonlinear moving validation.** Compare the proposed bias-plus-harmonic or reduced model with at least one nonlinear motion-transient case near the B-H knee. If core loss matters, require separately identified loss/hysteresis material data and an appropriate reference. Demonstrate energy balance with all required electrical, mechanical and field-storage terms.
7. **Useful desktop delivery.** Measure job runtime/memory and cache-hit latency on representative geometries. Validate reduced models at held-out positions, currents and speeds. Require uncertainty/out-of-envelope labeling and a stable failure path. No claim of interactive full 3D speed should precede this measurement.

Recommendation after these gates: select one external backend only if its accuracy, deployment and recurring costs pass. Keep it optional and use its accepted results to improve bounded fast estimates. If procurement fails, test GetDP for the same narrowly specified reference problems before starting a full Rust implementation. Build a native 2D solver only against a written restricted scope; do not present it as the eventual 3D answer by default.

### Gaps

- These gates have not been run. Existing FEMM results remain the available planar evidence, and independent 3D or hardware drag evidence is still missing.
- There is no supported basis for a precise build-versus-integrate timeline. Integration retains substantial model and validation work, but removes the need to invent and maintain most numerical machinery. A bespoke solver has the highest technical uncertainty; 2D restriction reduces it without eliminating material and force-validation risk.
