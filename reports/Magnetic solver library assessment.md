# Keep magba; require proven material solvers

**Do not add fenris or sim-nest/sim-femm for this task. Keep magba as the prescribed-source analytical B-field backend, and retain real FEMM as the independent offline 2D reference.** Magba is sufficient for that narrow role, not for self-consistent magnetic materials or eddy currents. The new backplane calculations belong to the application, and their validation does not extend to the complete 3D motor. Recorded thin-sheet saturation cases pass, but thick plates near the B-H knee overpredict attraction by approximately **14–52%**. Harmonic eddy-current cases agree closely in 2D; finite-depth 3D drag remains unvalidated. Neither researched Rust FEM library supplies the missing, demonstrated electromagnetic capabilities. These conclusions synthesize source reviews and recorded project results as of 2026-10-18; no installation, build, or simulation was performed. ([Magba API](https://docs.rs/magba/0.7.2/magba/base/trait.Source.html), [project validation](../scripts/femm/RESULTS.md#L382-L451))

## Neither Rust FEM library delivers the missing physics

Fenris explicitly targets low-level FEM, especially solid mechanics. It implements nodal triangles, quadrilaterals, tetrahedra and hexahedra, but the reviewed operators, materials and examples establish no magnetic B-H model, magnet excitation, eddy-current formulation or magnetic force benchmark. Its nonlinear materials are mechanical laws. Core elliptic assembly uses real scalars, and the reviewed element interfaces provide no H(curl) edge-element implementation. These are bounded source-review findings, not proof that nobody has built a downstream solver. General extension interfaces could support new solver development, but **3D elements do not establish a 3D electromagnetic solver**. ([Fenris scope](https://github.com/InteractiveComputerGraphics/fenris/blob/7181b15684ad1eb90d95cc75f1566a8259a0241d/README.md), [operators](https://github.com/InteractiveComputerGraphics/fenris/blob/7181b15684ad1eb90d95cc75f1566a8259a0241d/src/assembly/operators.rs), [mechanical materials](https://github.com/InteractiveComputerGraphics/fenris/blob/7181b15684ad1eb90d95cc75f1566a8259a0241d/fenris-solid/src/materials.rs), [real elliptic assembly](https://github.com/InteractiveComputerGraphics/fenris/blob/7181b15684ad1eb90d95cc75f1566a8259a0241d/src/assembly/local/elliptic.rs#L361-L382), [element interfaces](https://github.com/InteractiveComputerGraphics/fenris/blob/7181b15684ad1eb90d95cc75f1566a8259a0241d/src/element.rs#L27-L50))

Fenris does contain CG and Newton utilities despite older README wording excluding system solving. That discrepancy should not obscure the actual deficiency: no delivered magnetic application or relevant validation. The latest observed public commit and `fenris 0.0.33` publication date to 2023-09-25. Adopting it would mean owning electromagnetic formulation, materials, boundaries, meshing integration, force extraction and verification, not integrating an established reference solver. ([CG](https://github.com/InteractiveComputerGraphics/fenris/blob/7181b15684ad1eb90d95cc75f1566a8259a0241d/fenris-sparse/src/cg.rs), [Newton](https://github.com/InteractiveComputerGraphics/fenris/blob/7181b15684ad1eb90d95cc75f1566a8259a0241d/fenris-optimize/src/newton.rs#L54-L87), [pinned commit](https://github.com/InteractiveComputerGraphics/fenris/commit/7181b15684ad1eb90d95cc75f1566a8259a0241d), [crate metadata](https://crates.io/api/v1/crates/fenris))

Sim-femm's name is particularly misleading for this decision. Its steady solve calls its own mesher and assembly, not FEMM. **Nonzero-frequency magnetic solves explicitly return `UnsupportedPhysics`.** Its advertised nonlinear path ignores the arbitrary `nu_of_b2` material expression, uses a hardcoded cubic residual, assembles its derivative at zero state, and then supplies a constant assembled linear residual/Jacobian to PTC. Thus the inspected path does not implement a field-updated arbitrary B-H solve. This conclusion follows from source inspection, not a numerical reproduction. ([Own solve path](https://github.com/sim-nest/sim-femm/blob/dcc1205664cb74fc77aab7d62ae650cd2c0ac759/crates/sim-lib-femm-solve/src/steady.rs#L52-L301), [harmonic rejection](https://github.com/sim-nest/sim-femm/blob/dcc1205664cb74fc77aab7d62ae650cd2c0ac759/crates/sim-lib-femm-physics/src/implementation.rs#L170-L200), [zero-state assembly](https://github.com/sim-nest/sim-femm/blob/dcc1205664cb74fc77aab7d62ae650cd2c0ac759/crates/sim-lib-femm-assembly/src/implementation.rs#L249-L263))

Other source observations independently block adoption. Sim-femm uses 2D P1 triangles; its default fan mesher assigns the first block label throughout, and boundary validation rejects non-Dirichlet kinds. Its named energy, force and Joule-loss quantities use formulas that do not establish physical electromagnetic outputs: energy integrates squared mean potential, and loss integrates squared potential gradient without conductivity or frequency. Inductance derives from that energy rather than a bias-linearized solve. Assembly allocates a dense `n*n` matrix before CSR conversion. Passing CI, sensitivity comparisons or algebraic convergence certificates cannot substitute for solved-field benchmarks. ([Meshing](https://github.com/sim-nest/sim-femm/blob/dcc1205664cb74fc77aab7d62ae650cd2c0ac759/crates/sim-lib-femm-mesh/src/implementation.rs#L229-L292), [boundaries](https://github.com/sim-nest/sim-femm/blob/dcc1205664cb74fc77aab7d62ae650cd2c0ac759/crates/sim-lib-femm-mesh/src/validation.rs#L75-L84), [postprocessing formulas](https://github.com/sim-nest/sim-femm/blob/dcc1205664cb74fc77aab7d62ae650cd2c0ac759/crates/sim-lib-femm-post/src/quantity.rs#L191-L380), [dense assembly](https://github.com/sim-nest/sim-femm/blob/dcc1205664cb74fc77aab7d62ae650cd2c0ac759/crates/sim-lib-femm-assembly/src/implementation.rs#L224-L281), [CI](https://github.com/sim-nest/sim-femm/blob/dcc1205664cb74fc77aab7d62ae650cd2c0ac759/.github/workflows/ci.yml))

## Magba is sufficient for sources, not material response

Magba's `Source` evaluates B from specified sources. Its `MeshMagnet` is a prescribed-polarization boundary geometry, not a material FEM mesh. Neither that API nor the cuboid parameters supply permeability, conductivity, nonlinear B-H response or self-consistent magnetization. The application separately integrates coil Lorentz force and torque, constructs image magnets and computes layered material reflection. Consequently, correcting saturation does not require replacing magba's cuboid field kernel. Agreement with Magpylib is useful analytical regression evidence, but magba acknowledges shared analytical origins, so that comparison is not independent validation of soft-material physics. ([Source](https://github.com/p-sira/magba/blob/8a3628eb52ebfeb271caadb1c87d5d495446d05b/src/base/source.rs#L16-L49), [mesh magnet](https://github.com/p-sira/magba/blob/8a3628eb52ebfeb271caadb1c87d5d495446d05b/src/magnets/mesh.rs#L15-L50), [cuboid](https://github.com/p-sira/magba/blob/8a3628eb52ebfeb271caadb1c87d5d495446d05b/src/magnets/cuboid.rs#L10-L43), [native force integration](../crates/pcbmotorgen-simulation/src/magnetic/force_eval/mod.rs#L476-L503), [backplane assumptions](../crates/pcbmotorgen-simulation/src/backplane/mod.rs#L7-L99), [analytical origins](https://docs.rs/magba/0.7.2/magba/#acknowledgment))

The independent FEMM results define the usable envelope. Thin ferrite at 0.1, 0.2 and 1 mm passes recorded tolerances, with field ratios within 1.4% and attraction approximately 6–7% high. Thick near-knee attraction is **51.9% high for 5 mm flexible ferrite and 13.5% high for 2 mm MnZn**. The failure persists with the model's own two-slope B-H curve supplied to FEMM. The recorded diagnosis is omitted normal-field saturation at the face, while the model saturates from in-plane field. Matching another scalar permeability or knee shape therefore does not establish a correction. ([Nonlinear comparisons](../scripts/femm/RESULTS.md#L382-L413), [failure diagnosis](../scripts/femm/RESULTS.md#L453-L471))

The 100 kHz linear harmonic checks agree within 0.5% for the real coil factor and 1.3% for added resistance; traveling-wave 2D drag agrees within 2.1% across tested 1–100 m/s cases. These checks use fixed material properties, including chosen effective permeabilities for saturated cases. They validate the stated 2D harmonic equations, **not nonlinear dynamic ferrite response or real 3D drag**. Finite 5 mm magnet depth, array-end currents, coverage weighting, temperature-dependent conductivity and hysteresis/core loss remain outside demonstrated validation. New layered-sheet cases also lack a repeated mesh-convergence study. Treat the reported 0.86 N drag at 20 m/s as a screening warning, not an accepted 3D operating prediction. ([Harmonic results](../scripts/femm/RESULTS.md#L423-L451), [unverified effects](../scripts/femm/RESULTS.md#L478-L517), [mesh-check scope](../scripts/femm/RESULTS.md#L45-L50), [operating estimate](../scripts/femm/RESULTS.md#L327-L353))

## Require demonstrated 3D physics before accepting a backend

A future solver should pass a written acceptance specification before integration. Require measured, provenance-tracked material curves; vector-field nonlinear bias including normal-face saturation; and incremental harmonic response about that bias, with the differential tensor where required. Require permanent-magnet excitation, correct SI normalization, multiregion meshing and explicit boundary/gauge treatment. Its 3D conducting formulation must resolve skin depth, current closure, finite magnet depth and array ends, with a documented motion formulation appropriate to the case. These are proposed requirements addressing the observed omissions, not capabilities established for any unreviewed package. ([Current saturation failure](../scripts/femm/RESULTS.md#L461-L475), [coupling and 3D limits](../scripts/femm/RESULTS.md#L485-L517))

Acceptance should include the existing FEMM thin and failed thick-plate cases, harmonic impedance and traveling-wave tests, followed by finite-depth and end-effect sweeps against independent 3D references or measurements. Set numerical error budgets before testing; a reasonable proposed starting gate is less than 2% change under mesh and outer-domain refinement and less than 5% disagreement on reference forces, impedance and losses, subject to reference uncertainty and design needs. These thresholds are recommendations, not achieved results. Require separate mover and backplane forces, winding linkage or an inductance matrix, complex impedance, Joule loss, and energy balance, including drag power versus loss in an appropriate steady-motion case. Verify the coupled force convention rather than assigning all reversed coil reaction to the mover. Preserve geometry, material data, bias, speed/frequency, solver version and convergence evidence with every result. Finally, require a locked dependency/license audit, platform demonstration and measured runtime/memory on a representative motor mesh. ([Existing power checks](../scripts/femm/RESULTS.md#L437-L451), [separate force outputs](../scripts/femm/README.md#L56-L62), [existing force convention](../crates/pcbmotorgen-simulation/docs/API.md#L285-L300))

## Conclusion

The immediate engineering action is to flag or correct thick near-knee backplane predictions, not add another library under the assumption that FEM infrastructure supplies validated magnetic physics. Preserve distinct labels for analytical equivalence, independent 2D verification, independent 3D verification and hardware validation. That separation lets the fast Rust model remain useful without turning a successful planar benchmark into an unsupported motor-level accuracy claim.
