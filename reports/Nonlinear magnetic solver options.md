# Adopt mature solvers before building one

**Existing mature solvers can solve nonlinear magnetic saturation and induced eddy currents. We should not build a full native Rust solver yet.** Start with a headless **Elmer proof of concept**, because inspected implementation and regression inputs directly support 3D nonlinear magnetic materials, permanent magnets, conducting volumes, material velocity, harmonic circuits and force extraction. Keep GetDP with Gmsh as the fallback when explicit control of custom formulations matters; Sparselizard and NGSolve are credible alternatives. Adopting an engine still leaves us responsible for the motor model, material data, meshing, integration and coupled analysis workflow. **No candidate was verified ready out of the box for the complete nonlinear DC bias, differential-tensor harmonic response and moving finite-depth 3D motor workflow.** Maxwell or COMSOL can provide an independent commercial reference if budget permits, but buying one is not a prerequisite for starting. This is source research as of 2026-10-18, not a solver trial: no solver was installed or run. ([Elmer nonlinear test](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/tests/mgdyn_bh/case.sif), [3D solver implementation](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/src/modules/MagnetoDynamics/WhitneyAVSolver.F90), [GetDP manual](https://getdp.info/doc/texinfo/getdp.html))

## The local failures require vector saturation and real 3D conductors

The present analytical backplane model overpredicts attraction by **51.9% for 5 mm flexible ferrite and 13.5% for 2 mm MnZn**, approximately +14–52%. Supplying its own two-slope B-H law to FEMM leaves essentially the same force errors. The recorded diagnosis is missing saturation from the normal field at the plate face, while the approximation treats in-plane saturation. This calls for a self-consistent vector-field solve, not another fitted scalar permeability. Thin-sheet cases behave better, so the fast approximation remains useful within a stated envelope. These are recorded project comparisons, not new measurements or hardware validation. ([Local saturation evidence and diagnosis](../scripts/femm/RESULTS.md#saturation-and-eddy-currents-2026-10-18))

The eddy-current work has a different status. Recorded 100 kHz planar checks agree within 0.5% for the real coil factor and 1.3% for added resistance; traveling-wave planar drag agrees within 2.1%. Those checks use fixed permeability, including selected effective values in saturated cases. **They support the tested 2D equations, not nonlinear dynamic ferrite response or finite-depth 3D drag.** Real 5 mm magnet depth, finite array ends and housing current-return paths remain unverified. The newer layered cases also lack a repeated mesh-convergence study. Keep magba for prescribed-source analytical fields and real FEMM for independent planar checks; neither supplies the missing full-motor verification. ([Local harmonic results and limits](../scripts/femm/RESULTS.md#coil-at-100-khz-2-sin-current-sheets-solved-alone-time-harmonic), [mesh-check scope](../scripts/femm/RESULTS.md#setup), [magba source API](https://docs.rs/magba/0.7.2/magba/base/trait.Source.html), [FEMM incremental analysis](https://www.femm.info/wiki/ReadMe42))

Elmer's advantage is unusually direct evidence for the mechanisms that our approximation omits. Its real solver includes nonlinear H-B treatment, magnetization and material velocity; moving material triggers scalar electric potential. Its Faraday-disc verification explicitly demonstrates charge redistribution until the electric field cancels the motional electromotive force. This is a useful negative test for naive local `J = sigma (u × B)` models, which can predict currents that cannot actually close. ([Real solver](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/src/modules/MagnetoDynamics/WhitneyAVSolver.F90), [Faraday-disc verification](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/tests/mgdyn_faraday_disc/Readme.txt))

There is an important limit to that recommendation. Elmer's harmonic implementation accepts a reluctivity tensor, but its ordinary nonlinear harmonic H-B branch evaluates a curve from the harmonic field magnitude. That is **not proof of an incremental solve around an independently computed PM/DC operating point**. GetDP documents the differential tensor explicitly, but its inspected inductor example disables ordinary nonlinear material selection in frequency-domain mode. Neither observation rules out a custom bias-linearized workflow; both rule out calling it already turnkey. Some Elmer evidence comes from mutable `devel` sources, so check every relied-on feature against a pinned release before implementation. ([Harmonic implementation](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/src/modules/MagnetoDynamics/WhitneyAVHarmonicSolver.F90), [GetDP nonlinear tutorial](https://getdp.info/dev/doc/texinfo/getdp.html), [inductor input](https://onelab.info/files/inductor/inductor.pro))

## Choose DC, incremental or transient analysis before running a solver

For standstill attraction and saturation, solve **nonlinear 3D magnetostatics** with permanent-magnet remanence, DC winding currents and the full vector B in the ferrite. Use real material curves for the actual grade, magnetic thickness and temperature. The flexible-sheet assumptions currently borrow bulk-material saturation values and include nonmagnetic layers in nominal sheet thickness. Better numerics cannot repair unidentified material properties. A single-valued B-H curve models anhysteretic saturation, not minor-loop hysteresis or frequency-dependent core loss. ([Local material assumptions](../scripts/femm/RESULTS.md#what-the-model-does-now), [Elmer nonlinear input](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/tests/mgdyn_bh/case.sif))

For sufficiently small ripple about a nearly stationary bias, solve **incremental harmonic magnetoquasistatics** after the nonlinear DC solve. For an isotropic reversible law `H = nu(b) B`, with `b = |B|`, the differential reluctivity at the bias is

`D = dH/dB = nu I + [nu'(b)/b] B₀ B₀ᵀ`.

Its longitudinal response is `dH/db`; its transverse response is `H/b`. A scalar secant permeability, or the longitudinal derivative applied in every direction, is not this tensor. Transfer the spatial tensor and its direction into the AC solve, with a regular zero-field limit. The DC permanent magnet is the bias source, not an oscillating PM excitation in the perturbation problem. Extract terminal `Z(omega) = V/I`, effective resistance `Re(Z)` and `L_eff = Im(Z)/omega`; use flux-linkage derivatives for low-frequency differential inductance. **Do not call `2W/I²` differential inductance in this biased nonlinear device.** The tensor workflow is proposed custom model work, grounded in the documented tangent and bias/perturbation distinction. ([GetDP tangent tutorial](https://getdp.info/dev/doc/texinfo/getdp.html), [FEMM incremental workflow](https://www.femm.info/wiki/ReadMe42), [COMSOL perturbation definition](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.comsol/comsol_ref_solver.36.059.html))

For large ripple crossing the saturation knee, appreciable changing bias during motion, or finite entry/exit effects, use **nonlinear transient analysis**, or a separately validated nonlinear periodic method. Independent linear harmonics only superpose within a demonstrated small-signal envelope. One 100 kHz solution does not represent arbitrary PWM. A slow nonlinear motion solution with local high-frequency perturbations is a useful candidate when time scales separate, but it needs comparison with a coupled transient case. Moving magnets can produce a large changing bias even when PWM ripple is small. ([Bias/perturbation workflow](https://www.femm.info/wiki/ReadMe42), [nonlinear moving transient formulation](https://ansyshelp.ansys.com/public/Views/Secured/Electronics/v252/en/Subsystems/Maxwell/Content/Maxwell/3DTransientExcitationsSources.htm))

The conducting solve must enforce current continuity throughout the **actual finite 3D volumes**, including ends, slots, insulation, leads and vias where relevant. A typical moving-conductor formulation uses `J = sigma(-∂A/∂t - grad(phi) + u × B)`, with a consistent reference frame and no double-counted motion. Resolve skin depth and proximity effects by convergence, rather than imposing a universal layer count. Fixed-geometry velocity terms suit only a frame in which the geometry/material distribution is stationary; a finite housing passing finite magnets generally needs position-dependent or transient geometry treatment. Static position snapshots alone do not solve induced currents. Thin-sheet or impedance-boundary reductions should follow finite-thickness validation, not replace it at the outset. ([Elmer A-v implementation](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/src/modules/MagnetoDynamics/WhitneyAVSolver.F90), [3D charge-conserving induction example](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/examples/magnetodynamics-av-induction-3d/main.cpp), [Elmer surface-impedance release notes](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/releases/tag/release-9.0))

## Adopt the numerical engine and own the application workflow

Use a headless external job rather than starting with Rust FFI. A practical prototype generates Gmsh geometry and physical tags, converts the mesh through ElmerGrid, writes an Elmer SIF and invokes ElmerSolver. Confirm exact command flags and compatible mesh formats against pinned versions. GetDP provides an alternative file-based CLI workflow. Keep the first prototype serial and user-installed on Linux; qualify Windows and macOS separately. Elmer documents those platforms, but its macOS build instructions warn that they can be outdated. No native signed Apple Silicon package, startup behavior or desktop performance was verified here. ([Gmsh manual](https://gmsh.info/doc/texinfo/gmsh.html), [ElmerGrid conversion](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/elmergrid/README.md), [Elmer distribution paths](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/README.adoc), [macOS instructions](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/compilation_instructions/macOS.md), [GetDP CLI tutorial](https://getdp.info/dev/doc/texinfo/getdp.html))

Our integration must own SI units, winding connectivity, magnetic versus adhesive thickness, material provenance, electrical contacts, mesh quality and boundary conditions. It must also orchestrate bias, tensor transfer, frequency/current/position sweeps, motion and circuit coupling. Return separate mover, coil and backplane forces, linkage or incremental inductance matrices, terminal impedance and losses by region. Do not assign every reversed coil reaction to the mover when the backplane exchanges force too. Store solver/version, geometry/material hashes, temperature, bias, excitation, mesh settings and convergence logs with every result. Failed or unconverged jobs must not become plausible-looking numeric answers. These are proposed integration requirements, not features implemented by this research.

Review distribution terms before bundling. Elmer's solver libraries and modules have mixed terms, and the inspected harmonic file itself contains both LGPL and GPL headers. GetDP and Sparselizard are GPL; NGSolve/Netgen use LGPL core terms. Audit each selected component and dependency, including meshers and sparse solvers. **A separate process is an engineering boundary, not an automatic copyleft exemption.** Preserve magba for fast previews and use accepted FEM results for bounded calibration tables or reduced models, with extrapolation warnings. ([Elmer policy](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/license_texts/ElmerLicensePolicy.md), [harmonic source headers](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/src/modules/MagnetoDynamics/WhitneyAVHarmonicSolver.F90), [GetDP terms](https://getdp.info/#Licensing), [Sparselizard license](https://github.com/halbux/sparselizard/blob/master/LICENSE), [NGSolve license](https://github.com/NGSolve/ngsolve/blob/master/LICENSE))

## Make adoption depend on staged evidence, starting with the failed attraction cases

**The next practical step is a reproducible Elmer nonlinear PM/ferrite force study, not full PWM plus motion.** First reproduce relevant upstream regressions, then establish the no-ferrite analytical limit and recover the known thin and failed thick planar cases. Extend to actual magnet depth only after that foundation. The following gates are proposed acceptance criteria, not achieved tolerances or promises of universal accuracy.

| Phase | Test and acceptance evidence |
|---|---|
| **1. Reproducible nonlinear bias** | Archive geometry, mesh tags, real B-H data and pinned solver. Recover linear/free-space limits and FEMM thin-sheet results; reproduce 5 mm flexible-ferrite and 2 mm MnZn references, including normal-face saturation without scalar fitting. Compare deep/periodic geometry with planar FEMM before attributing finite-depth differences to errors. |
| **2. Converged force and material sensitivity** | Refine air domain, air gap, ferrite thickness and force integration independently. A starting numerical gate is **less than 2% change** in integrated force, impedance and loss under refinement, then **less than 5% reference disagreement** where reference uncertainty permits. Set absolute tolerances near zero and a defined peak-B sampling rule instead of demanding a converged ideal sharp-corner maximum. Separate material uncertainty from discretization error. |
| **3. Conductor closure and skin response** | Recover an analytic or published skin/eddy benchmark and the established 2D harmonic limits. Run the Faraday-disc cancellation test, inspect 3D current-return paths, terminal conservation and closed-versus-slotted conductors. Check terminal power against integrated loss with explicit peak/RMS conventions. |
| **4. Finite-depth motion drag** | Recover a deep/periodic traveling-wave limit, then model actual 5 mm magnet depth, finite array ends and finite housing. Sweep relevant widths, lengths, thicknesses and speeds. Reverse velocity, test zero velocity and compare with independent 3D results or measurements. Use drag power versus loss only in a compatible steady passive case; include terminal power and field-energy change otherwise. |
| **5. Biased harmonic and nonlinear motion coupling** | Finite-difference-check the spatial differential tensor. Compare incremental linkage with nonlinear DC derivatives and small-amplitude transient results at several bias positions. Sweep ripple amplitude/frequency; reject harmonic superposition outside the agreed error budget. Compare at least one near-knee nonlinear moving transient with any proposed slow-bias/fast-ripple approximation. |
| **6. Desktop viability** | Measure runtime, peak memory, degrees of freedom and failure rates on representative geometries. Demonstrate cancellation, supported platforms and license compliance. Validate cached/reduced models on held-out points before deciding whether FEM belongs in the user-facing workflow or remains an offline reference tool. |

These gates target the recorded failures and missing tests, while upstream cases provide starting checks rather than motor-level validation. No runtime, memory, safe ripple threshold or completion schedule can be justified from source inspection alone. Real B-H curves, temperature-dependent conductivity and separate hysteresis/core-loss data remain required inputs when those effects matter. ([Local failures and outstanding limits](../scripts/femm/RESULTS.md#saturation-and-eddy-currents-2026-10-18), [Elmer nonlinear regression](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/tests/mgdyn_bh/case.sif), [closure verification](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/tests/mgdyn_faraday_disc/Readme.txt), [harmonic circuit regression](https://github.com/ElmerCSC/elmerfem/blob/devel/fem/tests/circuits_harmonic_massive_flux_linkage/sif/1962.sif))

## Conclusion

The decision is about which work to own. Adopt established numerical machinery and invest in the motor-specific model, materials and verification. Reconsider a native solver only after a measured prototype exposes a deployment, licensing or repeated-workload requirement that an external engine cannot meet. A narrowly scoped native 2D solver could then be a separate product choice, but it would not settle finite-depth drag. Until the 3D gates pass, label results distinctly as analytical estimates, independent 2D comparisons, independent 3D comparisons or hardware validation.
